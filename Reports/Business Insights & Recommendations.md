# Business Insights

**Things to Note:**
- A complaint is a consumer-reported issue, not proven misconduct.
- A company response category is a recorded classification, not proof of a satisfactory outcome.
- 2017 and 2023 are partial years.

---

### Insight 1: Complaint volume grew 64.5% between 2018 and 2022 and is still rising in 2023
- Full-year volumes rose from 7,872 (2018) to 12,953 (2022), an increase of 64.5%. The only decline in that run was in 2019 (−10.12%).
- On a like-for-like basis (1 Jan–28 Aug), 2023 recorded 9,131 complaints, 11.34% more than the same period in 2022 (8,201), the highest of any year.
- **Meaning:** demand on complaint handling has grown every year since 2019. The 2023 partial-year total (−29.51% vs 2022) understates this, and should not be read as a decline.

### Insight 2: Two products account for two-thirds of all complaints
- Checking/savings accounts (39.69%) and credit/prepaid cards (25.91%) together account for 65.60% of complaints. Adding credit reporting and mortgages brings this to 88.49%.
- Managing an account is the single most common issue, having 15,109 complaints (24.17% of all complaints). Within checking/savings it makes up 60.89% of complaints, ahead of closing (11.90%) and opening (10.98%) accounts.
- **Meaning:** improvement effort aimed at day-to-day account servicing would affect the largest share of complaints.

### Insight 3: Timeliness dropped sharply in mid-2021, and 2021 accounts for half of all untimely responses
- Overall, 96.06% of complaints with a recorded value were marked timely. In 2017–2019 the rate was above 99.8% each year, but in 2021 it fell to 89.02%.
- The drop starts abruptly: June 2021 had a timely rate of 58.35% (389 untimely out of 934). It stayed below 85% for most of Jun–Oct 2021.
- 2021 holds 1,224 of the 2,403 untimely responses (50.94%) across the whole period.
- In 2021 the untimely rate was highest for debt collection (19.52%) and credit reporting (18.57%). Mortgage stayed at 0.42%.
- Timeliness recovered only partly: 95.62% in 2022 and 93.16% in 2023, still below the pre-2020 level.
- **Meaning:** the data shows *when* and *where* timeliness failed, but not *why*. The cause needs internal investigation.

### Insight 4: CFPB forwarding is usually same-day, but referral complaints make up nearly half of the long delays
- The median forwarding interval is 0 days and the average is 1.22 days. Only 435 complaints (0.70%) took more than 30 days, with a maximum of 275.
- Referral complaints average 3.30 days and only 24.41% are forwarded the same day, compared with 92.22% for web complaints. Referrals are 17.22% of volume but 47.59% of all complaints taking more than 30 days.
- Among the larger products, mortgage has the highest average interval (1.86 days) and 1.41% of mortgage complaints (93) exceed 30 days.
- **Meaning:** these delays happen before the company receives the complaint. They should not be counted against the company's response performance.

### Insight 5: July 2023 is the highest month on record, driven by checking/savings
- July 2023 recorded 1,749 complaints, 57.45% above the Jan–Jun 2023 monthly average of 1,110.8, and the highest of all 76 months.
- Checking/savings complaints alone were 883 in July, more than double June's 423.
- **Meaning:** this is an early-warning signal. The dataset can't explain the cause, but a spike this size warrants a targeted review.

## What This Analysis Cannot Reveal
- *Company response or resolution time.* There is no company response date, and the forwarding interval is CFPB-side only.
- *Complaint rates.* There are no customer or account counts, so volumes cannot be normalized by size.
- *Why* timeliness dropped in 2021 or volume spiked in July 2023. Those causes need internal data.
- Whether complaints were justified, or whether consumers were satisfied with the response.
